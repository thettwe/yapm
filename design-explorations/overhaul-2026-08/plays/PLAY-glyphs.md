# PLAY — glyphs: a mark language only yapm could mean

**The idea.** Stop borrowing Linear's icon vocabulary and draw one from yapm's own thesis.
Status becomes *position in the loop*: one circle, progressively arc-filled — dotted (backlog),
empty (todo), half arc (in progress), three-quarter (in review), quiet fill (done), slashed
(canceled). The track-break `//` is formalized as the brand mark — the same 70° stroke that
cancels a loop and breaks a delivery track also signs the wordmark. Around those two gestures,
a 26-mark working set with one grammar: dots are facts, rings are promises, arcs are progress,
ticks are weight, the slash is the difference between them. One 1.6px stroke, one 20-grid,
nothing points, blinks, or spins.

**Why it is yapm's.** Every mark restates a product position: teams is two rings whose *overlap*
is shaded (team-level always), triage is many-above-the-bar-one-let-through, delivery is a dot
standing on the ground it shipped to, priority is tick *count* not bar *volume*. The applied page
proves recognition cost: the issue list is pixel-identical to horizon/issue-list-deck except the
marks, and it still cold-reads in one pass.

**Graduate:** the status-as-arc set (it survives 14px and the group headers), the `//` mark +
wordmark lockup, priority ticks with ghost ticks, the track-node grammar (dot/ring/half/square),
and the "rules of the language" paragraph as the icon-contribution guideline.
**Stay a sketch:** retro/inbox/projects/roadmap marks (weakest shapes, least product argument),
the construction band (specimen theater), and the Cycles day-dot until it's tested against a real
14-day ring.

- `glyphs-specimen.html/png` — self-critique: the working-set grid holds 18 cells calmly, but
  retro still leans on a generic loop-arrow and "Change open" only reads with its caption.
- `glyphs-applied.html/png` — self-critique: status-as-arc is learnable in a day precisely
  because it changes almost nothing at reading distance — which also means the play's boldness
  lives in the specimen, not the product shot.
