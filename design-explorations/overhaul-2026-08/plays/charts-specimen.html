Chart vocabulary

Five drawn forms for Delivery, journalism grade — every chart keeps its own words. team-level only · never a per-person number

01

Annotated cycle timeline

Annotation is first-class ink. The chart carries its own captions — on the chart, in plain words; dates ride below in mono.

Cycle 2 as a band, Jul 30 → Aug 12. ● a release reached production · ○ merged but not reflected on the board.

02

Distribution strip

Never summarize what you can show. Every merged change is a dot; the median is a claim you can check against the dots behind it.

Open → merged for the last 26 changes, one dot each. The scale is linear — the crowd at the left is the story. ○ the two that waited 200h+.

03

Flow band v2

Ribbons must survive a glance: tinted fill, a real edge, and the count printed on the ribbon itself. Canceled work is drawn hollow — let-go, not colored.

What each cycle shipped, carried into the next, or canceled (hollow). Ribbons follow carried work forward; the number rides the ribbon.

04

Review rhythm

Small multiples, one tiny life per merged change: pattern-readable as a team, anonymous as a row. Shared scale; the tail is clipped, never hidden.

Each line is one merged change: sand waiting for the first look → violet in review (dots are rounds) → ● merged. Scale runs 0–96h; → ran past it. No names, ever.

05

The spark vocabulary · 64 × 18

Five row-sized marks, one grammar. A spark never gets an axis, a label, or a second series — it earns its place by shape alone.
